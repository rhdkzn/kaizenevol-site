Drive-through car washes are everywhere, they're cheap, and they take three minutes. Mobile car valeting takes a few hours and costs more. So why do so many Bristol drivers make the switch — and never go back? The answer is in what actually happens to your car in each scenario.

What Actually Happens in a Drive-Through Car Wash

Automatic car washes work on a simple principle: move quickly and clean everything at once. Those large rotating brushes or cloth strips are shared between hundreds of cars a day — and they carry the grit, debris, and contamination from every vehicle that went before yours.

That debris, spinning against your paintwork at speed, creates swirl marks and fine scratches in the clearcoat. These are often invisible in overcast conditions but become highly apparent in direct sunlight — showing up as circular scratches across the entire surface, particularly on darker-coloured cars.

Touchless washes avoid the physical contact problem but compensate with harsh alkaline chemicals that strip paint protection, dull finishes, and accelerate paint oxidation over repeated use. They're marginally better for the paint surface, but still a long way from what proper care looks like.

What a Professional Mobile Valet Actually Involves

A mobile valet session is slower by design. Every stage is deliberate, and every product is chosen to protect the surface rather than simply shift visible dirt.

At ADZ Valeting, a professional session starts with a pre-wash — a contactless stage using traffic film remover and, where needed, an iron fallout decontaminant. This breaks down bonded contamination before anything touches the paint. By the time the wash phase begins, the paint surface is already free of the abrasive particles that cause scratches in a standard wash.

The wash itself uses the two-bucket method with pH-neutral shampoo and dedicated wash mitts — never sponges. After rinsing, a clay bar treatment removes any remaining embedded contamination that even the best pre-wash can't shift. The result is a surface that's genuinely clean, not just visually clean.

The session closes with a protective product — wax, sealant, or a longer-lasting coating depending on the package chosen — that repels water, contamination, and Bristol weather between appointments.

The Hidden Maths

A weekly drive-through wash at £8–£12 costs between £416 and £624 per year. A professional mobile valet every 6–8 weeks — which includes decontamination, safe washing, and paint protection — typically costs less annually, and actively improves your car's condition rather than degrading it.

When you factor in the cost of paint correction work to address swirl marks accumulated over years of automatic washing, the drive-through option becomes the significantly more expensive choice.
